Schengen Gate Buddy

Support

Schengen Gate Buddy — Gate Buddy on your Home Screen — is an iPhone app from Game Pac that estimates your days under the Schengen 90/180 rule from the dates you enter: your trips, your visas and your long-stay permits. It is free, with no ads and no account, everything you enter stays on your device, and it requires iOS 26 or later.

This page was last revised on 5 October 2026.

Quick help

Enter and check trip dates

You log when you arrive and when you leave, and the app tells you the last day you can stay. A trip is two taps — arrived, and left; the date is read at the moment you tap, and you can change it. Add a future trip and the answer is instant: a trip that would break the rule is marked before you go. Tap “Why this number?” on the answer to see the window dates, each stay inside the window, and when old days drop out.

Reminders in Settings

Reminders are optional and are switched on in Settings. When you enable them, the app asks iOS for permission to show notifications, then schedules local notifications on your device — 14, 7, 3 and 1 days before your leave-by date, and on the day itself. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Export and import (JSON)

Export in Settings writes your data to a JSON file, and you choose where to save or share it. Import reads a JSON file you pick, shows what it contains, and replaces the app's records only after you confirm. If the file cannot be read or is not accepted, nothing changes.

Your data stays on your device

There is no account and no server: everything you enter is stored on your device. You can delete any trip, visa or permit inside the app, and deleting the app removes its data file and settings from the device. Apple's device backups may still hold copies under your own iCloud and device backup settings, and exported files stay wherever you saved them.

Estimates, not legal advice

Gate Buddy shows estimates, not legal advice. The app itself puts it this way:

“Estimates only. Gate Buddy counts days under the Schengen 90/180 rule from the dates you enter. It is not legal advice. It is not linked to the EU, Frontex or any government. Border officers decide how long you may stay, using the EU's Entry/Exit System (EES). If your EES record is missing a trip or an exit, keep proof such as boarding passes: you can use it to challenge the record. Before you rely on a date, check the EU's own tool at travel-europe.europa.eu. That tool ignores stays that began before 10 April 2026, but those days still count.”
